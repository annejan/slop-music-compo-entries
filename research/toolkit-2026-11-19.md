# Toolkit research, 19 November

What more can we prepare before the compo weekend (21 and 22 November): ACE-Step used better in our chain, other
singing and speaking voices, compo craft, and compo-day speed. Five research agents, one track each, then four
adversarial fact-checkers on the external claims (licences, does it run here without CUDA, install commands, our
rules). Nothing was installed or downloaded, and no GPU job ran: every speed for the Radeon 860M below is an estimate
unless it says "measured". Nobody has heard any of it yet; every item ends in an A/B for the user's ears.

## The headlines

1. **ACE-Step's key is a lottery, not a setting.** Key, tempo and metre reach the LM and the DiT only as text
   (one line in the LM's metadata block, one "# Metas" line for the DiT); nothing constrains the pitch, and an a
   cappella take never hears our chords. Of 166 takes on disk, 22 % were sung in the asked key (or its relative);
   "A minor", a "common key" per the docs, hit 19 %. So: make several cheap drafts, measure, shift the best one
   with Rubber Band, and let the ear choose.
2. **The time in a take is decoding, not loading.** A 37 s take: LM 15 s (load 1.7 s, 176 codes in 12 s), SFT DiT
   24 s (50 steps), VAE 7.6 s, all loads under 2 s (measured, from the take logs). A keep-loaded server buys
   almost nothing. The levers: several LM variations in one run (`lm_batch_size`), turbo drafts (8 steps) from the
   same LM codes, and SFT only for the take the user picks.
3. **The takes are not reproducible.** takes.py sets only `seed` (the DiT's noise); ace-lm draws a random
   `lm_seed` every run, and the tune comes from the LM. A changed request (or a lost WAV) silently makes a new take.
   Pin picked takes as files; put `lm_seed` in the request.
4. **Cut last words have a cause.** acestep.cpp truncates the duration to whole seconds and the LM stops about
   0.8 s early: 14 of 137 takes end loud. Ask for 1 s more and flag a loud ending.
5. **The harmony under a free take matters more than the take.** Today's Hi-NRG take sat on the kit's chord tones
   only 4-5 % of its long-note time as laid; one bar earlier, 75-77 %. The approved rounds had slower harmony
   under the take than today's chord-a-bar loop (whose F# major brought an A# against the A the take holds).
   Caveat from the fact-check: the samples are small (14 long notes), and Push It To Prod changes chord every bar
   too, so it is a hypothesis for an A/B, not a rule.
6. **Singing voice conversion runs here without PyTorch.** audio.cpp (Apache-2.0, ggml, Vulkan, the same kind of
   backend as acestep.cpp) runs Seed-VC (singing, 44.1 kHz), RVC and, since 17 November, SoulX-Singer (singing
   synthesis from a score and F0, and conversion). That is the route to "ACE's voice on OUR melody": our notes,
   sung in the voice of a 10-25 s ACE a cappella take (AI-made, so within the rules). The night's 64 per-genre
   takes (renders/ace/takes/genres) are a ready library of reference voices.
7. **Spoken voices: cheap Piper extras first, then voices made from a description.** Piper has Dutch voices
   (nl_NL pim, ronnie, alex; nl_BE nathalie, rdh, flemishguy; CC0) and many-speaker models for real gang shouts
   (en_US libritts_r, 904 speakers; en_GB vctk, 109; CC BY 4.0). For character (an MC who screams, a whispered
   house intro) the jump is an LLM TTS that designs a voice from text, with no recording of anyone: Qwen3-TTS
   VoiceDesign through qwentts.cpp (Vulkan, by the acestep.cpp author) or Maya1 (inline tags such as excited,
   whisper, scream, laugh) through llama.cpp Vulkan.

  repaint), so the output would be our instrumental with no vocals; and the user ruled lego out by ear.
- complete, extract, or a full ACE song followed by extract, as the route to fitting vocals.
- ACE-Step 1.5 on ROCm torch, ComfyUI's ACE nodes, the HOT-Step-CPP fork: a new runtime two days before the compo.

  audio.cpp).
- Heavy GPU TTS (Fish S2 Pro, Higgs Audio, Dia, IndexTTS, Zonos, VibeVoice) and older ones (Bark, MeloTTS,
  StyleTTS2, F5-TTS, Spark-TTS, XTTS-v2).
