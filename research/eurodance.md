# Eurodance (Sunday theme)

Goal: a track that sounds like 1992-1996 chart eurodance. Think big female
chorus, male rap verses, a synth stab riff, and a pounding four-on-the-floor
beat. The DJ must be able to mix it with the other entries.

## Tempo

- Wikipedia: four-on-the-floor at 120-150 BPM. From 1992 to about 1995 the
  sound became more NRG-driven, with tempos up to 150 BPM.
- One production guide gives 128-145 BPM. Another says 110-150, "usually 140".
- The verified reference tracks below sit mostly at 124-140 BPM. The core of
  the 1993-1995 peak is about 128-136 BPM.

**Advice for the compo:** ask for **132 BPM** if no target is given. Stay in
128-136 BPM. A track at 140+ BPM drifts into Eurotrance or happy hardcore. A
track at 124 or lower sounds like early "Rhythm Is a Dancer" style, which
mixes badly with 135 BPM tracks unless the DJ pitches a lot.

## The classic formula

1. **Female chorus.** A strong, often belted female voice sings the hook. The
   hook carries the melody of the whole song.
2. **Male rap verses.** Short rap verses, often in accented English, about
   love, dancing, freedom or the night. Wikipedia lists this split (male rap
   verse, female chorus) as a defining trait.
3. **Synth stab riff.** A short, punchy chord-stab or lead-synth riff that
   repeats between vocal parts. It is the instrumental "hook". Guides call
   the riff the part the listener must remember.
4. **Simple syncopated bass.** Offbeat or octave bass under the stabs.
5. **Key change.** A late chorus moves up a semitone or a whole tone. This is
   common in Europop of the era. It is not in every eurodance hit, and I found
   no source that measures how common it is (unverified).
6. **Pop structure.** Verse-chorus form, 3:30 radio edit, plus a longer
   extended mix for clubs.
7. **Hedonistic lyrics.** Party, romance, dancing, "tonight".

## Keys and chord progressions

- Mostly minor keys (A minor, G minor, F minor and similar). A eurodance
  tutorial states the songs are "mostly written in minor keys".
- Short loops, 4 chords, one chord per bar or per two bars.

| Progression | In A minor | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| i - VI - III - VII | Am - F - C - G | the classic "epic minor" loop |
| i - VI - VII - i | Am - F - G - Am | driving, resolves home |
| i - VI - iv - v | Am - F - Dm - Em | example from a eurodance tutorial |
| i - VII - VI - VII | Am - G - F - G | rocking, good for rap verses |
| VI - VII - i | F - G - Am | pre-chorus lift |

## Drums and sound

- Four-on-the-floor kick, punchy and loud. 909-style kick and claps.
- Offbeat open hi-hat, 16th closed hats, snare rolls into choruses.
- Straight timing. No swing (unlike house).
- Synths: early-1990s digital ROM synths such as the Korg M1 (the exact gear
  per track is unverified). Bright stabs, "hoover" sounds, piano riffs,
  string pads.
- Crash cymbal on every 8 or 16 bar boundary.

## Song structure in bars (extended mix)

8-bar phrases. At 132 BPM one bar is about 1.8 s, so 16 bars is about 29 s.

| Section | Bars | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | 16-32 | drums only, then bass and riff. DJ mixes in here. |
| Riff | 8 | stab riff, full beat |
| Rap verse 1 | 16 | male rap |
| Chorus | 16 | female hook |
| Riff | 8 | |
| Rap verse 2 | 16 | |
| Chorus | 16 | |
| Breakdown | 8-16 | kick drops, pads, vocal hook teaser |
| Build | 8 | snare roll |
| Chorus (key change) | 16 | +1 or +2 semitones |
| Riff / chorus | 16 | |
| Outro | 16-32 | strip back to drums. DJ mixes out here. |

## Sub-styles

- **Classic eurodance (1992-1995):** Germany (Frankfurt, Berlin), Belgium,
  Netherlands, Italy. Snap!, Culture Beat, 2 Unlimited, Haddaway, Real McCoy,
  Dr. Alban, Cappella.
- **Dutch / Belgian eurodance:** 2 Unlimited, Twenty 4 Seven, 2 Brothers on the
  4th Floor, Technotronic. Good local flavour for a Dutch party.
- **Italodance (mid-1990s to mid-2000s):** grew directly out of eurodance in
  Italy. Positive and uplifting, piano melodies, "metallic" bassline, vocoder
  and pitch-corrected vocals. Eiffel 65, Gigi D'Agostino, Gabry Ponte, Prezioso
  & Marvin. About 128-140 BPM in the tracks here.
- **Bubblegum dance (1997-2000):** cartoon-like, childish or cheeky lyrics,
  very bright, high-pitched vocals, novelty hooks. Aqua, Vengaboys, Daze.
  About 130-138 BPM here.
- **Eurotrance-ish / trance-pop (1997-2000):** trance synths and supersaw-like
  leads with a pop vocal. Alice Deejay, Sash!. About 130-140 BPM.
- **Euro-NRG / "hard" eurodance:** faster, harder kicks, up to 150 BPM.
  Captain Jack, Masterboy.
- **Euro-reggae:** slower ragga-pop with euro synths, around 100 BPM. Mr.
  President "Coco Jamboo". Does not mix with the rest.

## Suno style-tag vocabulary

Suno filters artist names. Describe the sound instead.

**Genre words:** 90s eurodance, classic eurodance, euro dance, Euro-NRG,
Italodance, bubblegum dance, eurotrance, 1990s dance pop.

**Tempo and rhythm:** 132 BPM, four-on-the-floor, pounding kick, offbeat
hi-hats, snare rolls, straight timing, steady tempo.

**Synths:** synth stab riff, chord stabs, bright supersaw lead, 90s rave
stabs, hoover synth, house piano riff, string pads, octave bassline, offbeat
bass.

**Vocals:** powerful female vocal chorus, belting female diva, male rap verses,
accented English rap, call-and-response, catchy sing-along hook, vocoder
(for Italodance), high-pitched cute vocals (for bubblegum).

**Arrangement:** key change in final chorus, big breakdown, build-up, radio
hit, extended club mix.

**Mood:** euphoric, energetic, nostalgic, party, melancholic minor key.
